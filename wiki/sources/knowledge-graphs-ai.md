# Knowledge Graphs Ai

> Source: `knowledge-graphs-ai.md`

## Summary

Knowledge graphs represent information as entities (nodes) connected by typed relations (edges), with properties attached to entities. They are used in search, open data and enterprise knowledge graph projects, and an LLM Wiki can be read as a text-based knowledge graph.

## Categories

- [[Knowledge Management]]

## Key Points

- A knowledge graph has three building blocks: entities (nodes), relations (typed edges such as "works_at") and properties (attributes such as a name or a date).
- Examples: the Google Knowledge Graph behind search information panels, [[Wikidata]] as a free collaborative knowledge graph, and enterprise knowledge graphs for internal information.
- LLM-powered agents use knowledge graphs for structured reasoning.
- In an LLM Wiki, pages play the role of entities and wiki links play the role of relations.

## Conclusions

- An LLM can keep the graph structure current by creating, updating and cross-referencing pages as new sources arrive.

- Keeping graphs current requires continuous updates.
- Querying and reasoning over large graphs can be computationally expensive.
